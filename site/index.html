Health Access MCP · Apple Health for AI

Your Apple Health data, for your AI.

I built this because I was sending my AI screenshots of every swim. After every workout the app writes your Apple Watch workouts and health data as files into a folder you pick. Your AI reads them from there, through the MCP or just as plain files. And when it writes you the next session, one tap puts it on your Watch. Free, open source, no account, no server.


Watch the 44-second video on YouTube

Who this is for

You train with an Apple Watch and use an AI as a coach. Claude, Cursor, a local model, or ChatGPT through the files. You want it to see your real sessions, heart rate, sleep and weight, not just what you remember to type. That's the main use.
Or you have a human coach. Share the same folder with them and they get the real splits instead of a screenshot.
Plans back to the Watch. Your AI writes the session as a small JSON file and the app turns it into a custom workout, with the step names on your wrist. No importing files, nothing to set up on the Watch.
Not for charts. The app has none, on purpose. Your AI is the one reading this data, so the work went into the files, the MCP and the docs, not into screens.

How it works

What you do, what runs on its own, and what your AI does. Orange is you, green is automatic, blue is your AI.

Once, about five minutes: you
  1. Install the app: Health Access MCP on the App Store. Free
  2. Pick a folder. Anything the iOS Files app can see works: iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, Proton Drive, a NAS, local. The only rule: your AI needs to reach the same folder, so pick one that syncs to the computer your AI runs on.
  3. Allow Health access (read only, you pick the types) and notifications (just one kind, "Your AI wrote S27", when a plan arrives).
  4. Connect your AI. You paste one paragraph and it sets itself up from these docs, see below.
After every workout: automatic
  1. You finish a workout on the Watch.
  2. Next time your phone is unlocked, the app writes workouts/2026-05-14_0712_PoolSwim_7A1C3E90.json into the folder: distance, time, heart rate every few seconds, every interval with its name, every length with stroke and SWOLF. Runs, walks and hikes also get splits per km or mile. Sleep, resting heart rate, HRV and the rest of the day come once a day.
  3. The folder syncs to your computer.
  4. Your AI reads it. No app needed on that side.
Sometimes, one tap: you
  1. Rate the session on your Watch when it asks. How hard it felt is the one number the Watch can't measure for you. Apple exports your Effort score and the app picks it up, the app itself never asks.
  2. When your AI has written a plan, tap "Put on Watch" in the Import tab. Or switch on "Put new plans on my Watch automatically" once and you don't even need that tap.
Whenever you ask: your AI
  1. Reads sessions, vitals and trends through the MCP, or the JSON files directly. How the folder is laid out.
  2. Writes plans/S27.json, which shows up in the app within a minute.
  3. Also works without the MCP: the folder has briefing.md, a one-page summary any AI can read. Upload it to ChatGPT, drop it into a Claude Project or mail it to a coach.

Two limits, both from Apple. Health data can only be read while the phone is unlocked, so if you finish a workout with the phone in your bag, it exports the next time you unlock it. And the Watch keeps its own pool length setting, a plan can't change it.

Connect your AI

The app only gets the data out, the MCP is what your AI actually works with. That's why the app's setup ends with this step, and why these docs are written so your AI can read them and set itself up.

Paste this to your AI

Set up the Health Access MCP for me.
Docs for you: https://healthaccessmcp.com/llms.txt
The server runs with: npx -y healthaccess-mcp
It finds my Health Access folder on its own.
Register it in this app, then list my last 5 workouts.

That's all the AI side needs, and it's the same for everyone. llms.txt and llms-full.txt have the setup per client, the file formats, the tools and the plan format. The app copies this paragraph for you, with your folder's name in it in case the search needs a hint.

Or by hand

claude mcp add --scope user healthaccess -- npx -y healthaccess-mcp
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "healthaccess": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "healthaccess-mcp"]
    }
  }
}

The first line is for Claude Code, the second is the config entry for Claude Desktop, Cursor, LM Studio and most other apps. You need Node.js 18 or newer. The server looks for your folder in iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, Box and ~/Documents, or you point it there with --dir. Setup page per client: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, ChatGPT, local models.

What the AI gets

ToolDoes
list_workoutsCompact rows, newest first. One line per session, written for an LLM.
get_workoutOne session in full: every interval with its name, pace and heart rate, zone seconds, lengths, splits and elevation for runs, recovery heart rate, your effort rating. The heart-rate series only if asked.
get_vitalsResting heart rate, HRV, sleep, weight, steps, VO2max by day.
training_summaryPer week: volume, sessions, easy-zone share, mean effort, and the cardiac cost trend (beats per 100 m).
rate_workoutStores the effort you told the AI. The AI must ask you, never guess.
push_planChecks a session (labels under 40 characters, zones 1 to 5, or a pace range on runs and rides) and writes it to plans/. From there the app puts it on your Watch.
get_plan_statusWhether the app has put a plan on the Watch yet: pending, scheduled, failed or cancelled.
list_plansEvery plan in plans/ with its state, newest first.

Arguments, what each tool returns and how the derived numbers are calculated are in the tool reference. The server also exposes the index and every workout as MCP resources (healthaccess://index, healthaccess://workout/<id>) and the same data through the healthaccess command line.

The app, three tabs

Export, Import, Settings. Each tab says in one line what it does, with a status that's green when nothing needs you. The screenshots use made-up example data.

Ideas for your AI

Eleven prompts in two groups. They're just suggestions, you don't need them for the setup. None of them assumes a sport, the AI asks. Each one has the AI ask you first, then read the data, then act, because a plan written without asking how you feel is mostly a guess. Full text of all eleven.

Export: understand what your Watch recorded

Weekly check-in

Asks about the week, sleep, stress and anything hurting. Reads four weeks of training and two weeks of sleep, resting heart rate and HRV. Ends with one thing that went well, one thing to change and one question.

Explain my last session

Asks one question first: what did you want from it? Then explains the session in plain words, what went to plan, what didn't and what it means for next time.

Am I overdoing it?

Asks about sleep, motivation and stress. Reads resting heart rate, HRV and load over three weeks. Gives you its best guess, not a verdict, and tells you when to see a doctor instead of asking an AI.

How am I sleeping and recovering?

Asks how rested you feel and what changed lately. Reads four weeks of sleep, heart and breathing next to your training days. Points out one pattern and one thing to try.

What changed over the last months?

Asks what you hoped to improve. Compares the first and the last four of twelve weeks: three things that moved and one that didn't.

Prepare this for my coach or doctor

Asks who it is for and what they care about. Writes a one-page summary from the files, ready to send, without a diagnosis.

Plans: sessions from your AI onto the Watch

Plan my next session

Asks sport, place, day and time, how long, how the last one felt and the goal. Writes one session, with instructions on your wrist, for the time you said.

Plan my week

Asks which days you can train and what the week is for. Proposes the week as a table, mostly easy, and writes one plan per session after your yes.

Change my plan

Missed a day, feel tired, travelling? Suggests the smallest change that works, without doubling up, and rewrites the sessions still ahead.

Build up to a goal

Asks the goal and the date, tells you whether it's realistic, sketches the weeks and only writes the next one.

Make my workout a Watch workout

Paste a workout from a coach, a club or an app. It becomes a plan the Watch can run, with repeats and labels.

Common questions

How do I get Apple Health data into Claude?

Install the Health Access app on your iPhone, pick a folder that syncs to your Mac, allow Health access. After every workout the app writes a JSON file into that folder. Then register the server once: claude mcp add --scope user healthaccess -- npx -y healthaccess-mcp for Claude Code, or the JSON snippet for Claude Desktop. Claude can then list workouts, read a session, read vitals and write a plan. Without the MCP, the folder holds plain JSON and a one-page briefing.md you can drop into a Claude Project.

Does it work with ChatGPT?

Through the files, yes. Through the MCP, not today. ChatGPT connects to MCP servers at an HTTPS URL, on some plans only, and does not start a program on your Mac. Health Access MCP is a program that runs on your Mac and talks over stdio. So with ChatGPT you upload the briefing or the workout JSON, or paste what the healthaccess command prints. What is true today, and the two paths.

Where does my data go?

Into the folder you picked, and nowhere else. There's no server and no account. The folder syncs through the storage you picked, with your own account there, not ours. Usage statistics are off unless you turn them on, and even then they only carry event names and counts, never a health value, a file name or a path. The exact event names.

Why does it need my phone unlocked?

Apple only lets apps read Health data while the iPhone is unlocked. iOS wakes the app as soon as a workout is saved, but if the phone is locked in your bag the export waits until you unlock it again, and the app shows that it's waiting.

Can it write workouts to my Apple Watch?

Yes. Your AI calls push_plan, which writes plans/<id>.json into the folder. The app turns it into a WorkoutKit custom workout with the step names on your wrist and puts it on the Watch, scheduled for a date or through Apple's "Add to Watch" sheet. It works for twelve sports, from running and cycling to pool swims and rowing. Limits: step labels under 40 characters, step names need iOS 18, and the Watch keeps its own pool length setting.
